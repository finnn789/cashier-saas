## Facts verified by the orchestrator (do not re-audit; read only what you call)
- `FnbPaymentService.pay` (CASH / MANUAL_NON_CASH): `orders.lockTenant(...)`, then `current(...)` (loads and `refreshOrder`s the order), idempotency checks, `settle(...)` which marks the order paid (`SalesOrderEntity.confirmPaid` -> `requireNewUnpaid`), inserts the payment and deducts stock in the same transaction.
- `FnbOrderCancellationService.cancel` (`POST /api/v1/cashier/outlets/{o}/fnb/orders/{orderId}/cancel`, body `FnbCancelOrderRequest`, reason required) and `FnbDiscountService.grant` (`POST /api/v1/merchant/outlets/{o}/fnb/orders/{orderId}/discount`, OWNER only, body `FnbDiscountRequest`) both take `orders.lockTenant` first and then `refreshOrder`. Both call `SalesOrderEntity.cancel/applyDiscount`, which require a NEW + UNPAID order, otherwise `InvalidOperationException` (400). A paid order cannot be cancelled or discounted. A cancelled unpaid order deducts no stock.
- The order entity is `@Version`ed (`MutableBaseEntity`). `GlobalExceptionHandler` has no dedicated handler for optimistic-lock failures: only a generic `Exception` handler, so an uncontrolled race would most likely surface as a 5xx. Without the tenant lock in `pay`, a pay racing with cancel/discount can read the order before the other transaction commits; the loser then fails on the version check (5xx) instead of waiting and getting a clean 400/409.
- Mutant files already exist outside the repo for the orchestrator (M3/M4 in the scratchpad); you do not touch them and you do not run anything.

## Part 2 — new scenarios H and I (payment racing with cancel / discount), repeated rounds
Each scenario creates its own tenant/outlet/ingredient (recipe 50 g per unit, plenty of stock) and runs 15 ROUNDS; every round creates ONE fresh unpaid POS order (1 unit of the recipe product) and fires exactly two requests together through the existing `runInParallel` helper (fresh start gate each round, so the two requests are released at the same instant). Collect the outcomes of all rounds and assert per round; after the loop assert the totals.
- H (pay vs cancel): request 1 = CASH payment with a unique key and sufficient tendered amount (cashier token); request 2 = cancel with a reason (cashier token). Per round, the legal outcome set is EXACTLY: one of them is 2xx and the other is 4xx (400 or 409; assert `>= 400 && < 500`), never both 2xx, never both failing, never 5xx. Then verify the final state in the DB/API: if the payment won, the order is PAID and not CANCELLED, exactly ONE payment row and exactly ONE `SALE` movement exist for the order (-50 for the ingredient); if the cancel won, the order is CANCELLED and UNPAID, NO payment row and NO `SALE` movement exist for it. After the loop: ingredient balance == initial - 50 x (number of rounds the payment won), and `balance == SUM(movements)`.
- I (pay vs discount): request 1 = CASH payment with a large tendered amount (>= the undiscounted total) and unique key (cashier token); request 2 = discount (owner token), e.g. NOMINAL 1000 with a reason (copy the existing discount call from `FnbPayFirstFlowIntegrationTest`/the other fnb tests; do not invent the DTO). Legal outcome per round: the payment is ALWAYS 2xx; the discount is 2xx or `>= 400 && < 500`; never 5xx. Consistency: read the persisted order total and the payment (amount paid / change as stored): `change == tendered - order total` (the order total as persisted AFTER both requests); if the discount was 2xx the persisted total is the discounted total (undiscounted total minus the discount effect) and there is exactly one discount row, otherwise the persisted total equals the undiscounted total and there are no discount rows; exactly one payment row; exactly one `SALE` movement for the order.
- Observability: for A, B, H and I print ONE summary line per test via `System.out.println("[concurrency] ...")` with the counts of outcomes (for A and B the numbers of 201 and 409 responses; for H how many rounds the payment won vs the cancel won; for I how many rounds the discount was applied before the payment vs rejected). This shows whether the requests really overlapped. Do not assert on these counts (they are timing-dependent).
